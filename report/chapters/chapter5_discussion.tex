\section{Discussion and Limitations}
\label{chap:discussion}

The results presented in Chapter~\ref{chap:results} reveal critical insights regarding the capabilities and constraints of parameter-efficient fine-tuning for generative CAD models. While LoRA enables highly data-efficient adaptation---requiring as few as 600 samples and minimal computational overhead---its effectiveness is strictly bounded by the representations already present in the base model's feature space. 

%===============================================================
\subsection{Distribution Constraints and Model Architecture}
%===============================================================

Because LoRA acts as a distribution sharpener rather than a distribution shifter, adapters can only amplify and concentrate existing modes. Consequently, the base model must already contain distributed representations of the target geometric features for successful fine-tuning. This explains why simple geometric primitives fine-tuned more effectively than complex, irregular shapes that fell outside the base distribution. Temperature scaling during inference proved robust in maintaining decodability but failed to induce the generation of genuinely out-of-distribution features. Additionally, the limited geometry and edge vocabulary (2000 tokens) inherent to AutoBrep's Finite Scalar Quantization limits the shape variety representable when predicting specialized domains.

Beyond representational bandwidth, specific architectural choices within the provided AutoBrep checkpoint fundamentally constrain the fine-tuned model's utility for interactive design tasks. The checkpoint was trained unconditionally and with geometry tokens disabled. This setup precludes arbitrary subset conditioning: the model fails to generate valid B-Reps when prompted with non-consecutive topological tokens and exhibits ambiguous capabilities for standard autoregressive completion without causing generation failures. Without explicit conditional training objectives, the interdependency modeling between faces and edges remains suboptimal for constrained generation.

%===============================================================
\subsection{Methodological Trade-Offs}
%===============================================================

The architectural constraints of AutoBrep frame a broader trade-off in generative CAD. Diffusion-based methods, such as BrepGen, natively support continuous conditioning and flexible generation. However, they frequently produce invalid geometries when applied to high-complexity solids. In contrast, AutoBrep's autoregressive approach maintains high decodability and structural validity across a broad complexity scale but lacks the conditional malleability required for targeted design workflows. Furthermore, the discrete tokenization model is highly sensitive to overfitting under limited data regimes. As observed during training, divergence between training and validation loss reliably preceded model collapse, requiring tight calibration of learning rates.
