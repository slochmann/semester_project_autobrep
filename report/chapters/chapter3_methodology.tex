\section{Methodology}
\label{chap:methodology}

%===============================================================
\subsection{AutoBrep Model Architecture}
%===============================================================

The AutoBrep model is built on a transformer-based decoder architecture designed specifically for B-Rep generation. The model operates on a discrete token sequence representation of CAD geometry, enabling efficient parallel processing during training.

\par

\textbf{Key architectural components:}
\begin{itemize}
	\item \textbf{Tokenization Layer}: Converts continuous B-Rep geometry (UV-grids, bounding boxes) into discrete tokens
	\item \textbf{Transformer Decoder}: Multi-head self-attention and feed-forward layers
	\item \textbf{Position Embeddings}: Captures sequential dependencies in geometry
	\item \textbf{Vocabulary}: A learned codebook of geometric primitives
\end{itemize}

The model is pre-trained on a large corpus of CAD models and exhibits strong zero-shot generation capabilities. Inference proceeds autoregressively, generating one token at a time conditioned on previously generated tokens and any conditioning signals (e.g., complexity level, sketch inputs).

%===============================================================
\subsection{Low-Rank Adaptation (LoRA) Fine-tuning}
%===============================================================

To adapt the AutoBrep model---a decoder-only Transformer for autoregressive B-Rep generation---using LoRA, adapters are strategically inserted into the query and value projection matrices within each transformer layer. The model's input and output structure defines the dimensions for these insertions.

\par

\textbf{Input/Output Dimensions and Projection:}

The AutoBrep model processes token sequences where each token's embedding has dimension $d_{\text{in}} = 768$. In each attention layer, linear projections operate as follows:
\[
\begin{array}{rcl}
\text{Input sequence:} & [\text{batch}, \text{seq\_len}, \mathbf{768}] & \leftarrow d_{\text{in}} \text{ (embedding dim)} \\
& \downarrow & \\
\text{Projection:} & \text{to\_q, to\_v}: \mathbb{R}^{768} \to \mathbb{R}^{768} & \leftarrow \text{Linear: } \mathbf{W} \in \mathbb{R}^{768 \times 768} \\
& \downarrow & \\
\text{Output:} & [\text{batch}, \text{seq\_len}, \mathbf{768}] & \leftarrow d_{\text{out}} \text{ (projection output dim)}
\end{array}
\]

\par

\textbf{LoRA Adapter Insertion Points:}

Adapters are inserted in parallel to the frozen, pre-trained weights of the Query (\texttt{to\_q}) and Value (\texttt{to\_v}) projection modules within each of the 35 transformer layers:
\[
\begin{array}{ll}
\text{Original:} & \mathbf{z}_q = \mathbf{W}_q \cdot \mathbf{x} \\
\text{With LoRA:} & \mathbf{z}_q = \underbrace{\mathbf{x} \cdot \mathbf{W}_q }_{\text{frozen}} +  \underbrace{\frac{\alpha}{r} \cdot \mathbf{x} \cdot \mathbf{B} \cdot \mathbf{A} }_{\text{adapter}} \\
\text{where:} & \mathbf{A} \in \mathbb{R}^{r \times 768},\ \mathbf{B} \in \mathbb{R}^{768 \times r}, r \in \{4, 8, 16\}
\end{array}
\]

\par

\textbf{Configuration Parameters:}
\begin{itemize}
	\item \textbf{LoRA Rank} ($r$): Controls capacity--memory tradeoff; typical values $r \in \{4, 8, 16\}$
	\item \textbf{LoRA Alpha} ($\alpha$): Scaling factor for adapter output; effective scale $= \alpha/r$; typical ratios \{1,2,3,4\}, higher ratios give more weight to adapter
	\item \textbf{Target Layers}: Applied to query and value projection layers only (not key or output projections)
	\item \textbf{Number of Layers}: 35 transformer layers, 2 adapter modules per layer (to\_q and to\_v)
\end{itemize}

\par

\textbf{Parameter Efficiency Analysis:}

With rank $r=4$, each adapter pair $(\mathbf{A}, \mathbf{B})$ adds $4 \times 768 + 768 \times 4 = 6,144$ trainable parameters. Across 35 layers and 2 modules per layer:

\[
\text{Total trainable params} = 35 \times 2 \times 6,144 = \mathbf{430,080} \text{ parameters}
\]

This represents only $\sim$0.05\% of the total model size ($\sim$1B parameters in AutoBrep). In contrast, the full \texttt{to\_q} and \texttt{to\_v} projections contain $2 \times 35 \times 768^2 \approx 41.9$ million parameters, demonstrating the dramatic parameter efficiency of LoRA fine-tuning.

%===============================================================
\subsection{Data Preparation Pipeline}
%===============================================================

A custom data processing pipeline converts raw STEP files (CAD file format) into a structured parquet dataset compatible with AutoBrep tokenization:

\par

\textbf{Processing steps:}
\begin{enumerate}
	\item \textbf{STEP Loading}: Parse CAD solids using Open Cascade (occwl.io)
	\item \textbf{Feature Extraction}: Extract faces, edges, UV-grids, and bounding boxes
	\item \textbf{Geometry Normalization}: Normalize coordinates to the $[-1, 1]$ range
	\item \textbf{Serialization}: Convert numpy arrays to bytes for compact storage
	\item \textbf{Parquet Export}: Write train/validation splits to disk
\end{enumerate}

The resulting parquet dataset contains serialized geometry tensors (faces: $\approx 12 \times 10 \times 10 \times 3$ points, edges: $\approx 23 \times 10 \times 3$ points) with corresponding bounding box annotations and topology metadata.

\subsection{Training Setup}
%===============================================================

Fine-tuning is performed using the AdamW optimizer on the frozen base model with LoRA adapters as the only trainable parameters.

\par

\textbf{Hyperparameters:}

		Learning Rate & 1e-4--5e-5 & Lower rates recommended for small datasets to prevent overfitting \\

Training is monitored via Weights \& Biases (W\&B) for real-time loss tracking, learning rate schedules, and system metrics. Convergence is typically achieved when training loss plateaus (typical range: 0.8--1.0).

%===============================================================
\subsection{Inference and B-Rep Generation}
%===============================================================

During inference, the fine-tuned model generates B-Rep sequences autoregressively, conditioned on a complexity token and optional text/sketch inputs. The generation process is controlled via temperature sampling and top-$p$ (nucleus) sampling:

\par

\textbf{Sampling strategy:}
\begin{align}
p_{\text{sampled}} &= \text{top\_p}(p_{\text{model}}, \text{threshold}=0.95) \\
\text{token} &\sim \text{Categorical}(p_{\text{sampled}} / T)
\end{align}

where $T$ is the temperature parameter (lower $T$ yields more confident/deterministic outputs).

\par

\textbf{Inference parameters:}
\begin{itemize}
	\item \textbf{Number of Samples}: How many B-Reps to generate ($N \geq 10$ recommended)
	\item \textbf{Complexity Token}: Controls model's generation preference (14=simple, 17=random)
	\item \textbf{Temperature}: Sampling diversity control ($0.5$--$1.0$ typical)
	\item \textbf{Top-$p$ Threshold}: Nucleus sampling parameter ($0.90$--$0.95$ typical)
\end{itemize}

Generated sequences are converted back to CAD-compatible formats (e.g., STEP files) for evaluation and visualization.
